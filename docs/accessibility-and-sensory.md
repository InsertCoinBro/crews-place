# Accessibility and sensory foundation

## Player controls

- Sound, music, voice, and motion can be adjusted independently.
- A calm-mode option reduces animation, decoration, and sound.
- No essential instruction depends only on color, sound, reading, or fine motor precision.
- Games do not require a countdown unless a player chooses one.
- Progress can be saved locally without requiring an account.

## Interaction

- Use large targets with generous spacing.
- Keep controls in predictable locations.
- Accept touch, mouse, and keyboard input.
- Avoid surprise audio, flashing, screen shake, and automatic motion.
- Let players repeat instructions and retry without penalty.
- Prefer positive, specific feedback over loud celebration.

## Content

- Offer age-respectful themes; do not assume every autistic player is a small child.
- Use clear language and visual instructions.
- Test activities with autistic players and caregivers instead of assuming one design fits everyone.
